Chapter 03 · Pedagogy

Lessons that sound human first.

The content system is constrained to CEFR A1, Standard German used in Germany, practical situations, and a repeatable teaching rhythm designed for absolute beginners.

Daily content contract

Two vocabulary bridges, two conversations

Each Short introduces a small vocabulary set. One day later, its linked long lesson uses that vocabulary naturally in context. The formats are connected but have different teaching jobs.

SHORT · 9:16

Recognition & recall

  • Exactly three German words or verbs
  • English meaning and part of speech
  • Simplified English pronunciation on screen
  • No example sentences
  • 20–58 seconds; target about 35
LONG · 16:9

Comprehension & speaking

  • A practical beginner situation
  • 12 alternating conversation turns
  • Guided phrase teaching and tutor cues
  • Final uninterrupted German conversation
  • 270–330 seconds; target about 300

Spoken sequence

What the learner hears

Visual pronunciation support is deliberately silent. The speech model reads the real phrase, not the English approximation.

FIRST

English once

The tutor states the meaning naturally and briefly.

THEN

German twice

The authentic German phrase is spoken, paused, and repeated without time-stretching.

ON SCREEN ONLY

Pronunciation guide

Simplified English spelling remains visible but is never included in narration.

Safety & scope

General audience, tightly bounded examples

The channel is family-safe but not directed specifically at children. Lessons avoid personal data, real-person imitation, copyrighted dialogue, and high-stakes advice.

USE

Safe practical domains

Cafés, groceries, transit, directions, routines, basic workplaces, study, schedules, weather, restaurants, and neighbours.

EXCLUDE

High-stakes instruction

Medical, legal, immigration, political, financial, and emergency guidance—as well as hate, sexual, violent, or exploitative material.

Content approval is intentionally cheap

Corrections happen before voice synthesis and rendering. A revision request creates a new validated proposal; it does not spend media compute on rejected text.

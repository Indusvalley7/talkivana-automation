# Talkivana Language Learning Companion Profile

This profile adapts the teaching principles of Uriva's Language Learning Companion skill for
Talkivana's approved five-minute, absolute-beginner video format. Source methodology:
https://github.com/uriva/language-learning (reviewed 2026-10-25). Talkivana's channel,
safety, audio, and approval rules remain authoritative if a general tutoring recommendation
does not fit the video format.

Apply this profile to every generated pack and to the independent quality-review pass.

## Learner and level

- Design for CEFR A1 learners with little or no prior German.
- Use high-frequency Standard German and short, concrete sentences. Prefer survival phrases,
  present-tense verbs, basic questions, and polite everyday chunks.
- Keep each line to one clear communicative idea. Make almost all language immediately
  comprehensible and introduce only a small, useful stretch beyond what was previously taught.
- Avoid rare synonyms, idiom-heavy speech, abstract explanations, long subordinate clauses,
  literary phrasing, and grammar terminology.
- Teach nouns with a natural article where the phrase permits it. Prefer fixed useful chunks over
  isolated grammar tables.

## Five-minute lesson progression

Treat each linked Short and long video as one compact tutoring sequence:

1. **Context:** Establish who the speakers are, where they are, and the single practical outcome
   they need to achieve.
2. **Input:** Introduce three high-frequency focus items through accurate English meaning and
   natural Standard German.
3. **Guided practice:** Present the conversation line by line with brief, cheerful tutor guidance,
   time to repeat, and no spoken pronunciation spelling.
4. **Complete role-play:** Replay the finished conversation in German only, with distinct voices.
5. **Review:** Close with a concise recall of what the learner can now understand or say. Do not
   add another English or German replay after the final German-only conversation.

The structured content object supplies lesson language rather than rendering directions. Keep its
dialogue suitable for this progression and let the renderer apply the approved narration sequence.

## Natural-conversation contract

Before accepting either long lesson, silently read only its German lines from first to last as one
scene. Rewrite the dialogue unless every requirement below passes:

- Give the two speakers stable roles, a shared situation, and a clear goal.
- Build a complete arc: natural opening or context, request/question, relevant response,
  necessary follow-up or clarification, confirmation/resolution, and an appropriate closing.
- Make every turn respond to or advance the immediately preceding turn. Never assemble unrelated
  textbook sentences merely because they use the vocabulary.
- Make every utterance plausible for that speaker's role. A customer asks about or buys goods; a
  seller answers, confirms, and completes payment. A passenger asks for travel information; station
  staff provide it. Do not make a character state, ask, or command something merely to insert a word.
- Preserve all facts across turns: names, items, quantities, time, place, and speaker intentions.
- Do not introduce a question that is ignored, an answer without a question, unexplained topic
  changes, repeated greetings, or an ending before the practical goal is resolved.
- Keep one register throughout a scene. Do not mix `du` and `Sie` forms. Choose the relationship
  that real speakers in Germany would use in that situation.
- Use natural German chunks and collocations instead of translating English word by word. The
  English line must express the same meaning and tone, even when the word order differs.
- Use both speakers purposefully and alternate A/B exactly. Speaker A starts each 12-turn scene.
- Make the linked Short vocabulary genuinely useful to the scene and include it without forcing
  unnatural wording.
- Treat each vocabulary item's `german` value as the exact teachable surface form. It must appear
  as one contiguous phrase in the linked dialogue, including any article or inflection stored in
  that field. If this would sound unnatural, choose a different focus form before finalising.
- Never turn an infinitive into an artificial noun phrase just to pass exact matching. For example,
  a price question and a request to pay are separate natural intentions; use a line such as
  `Ich möchte zahlen` rather than an invented construction such as `das Zahlen`.
- Select focus vocabulary because it is central to the scenario. Each item should support a real
  learner need in that scene, not force the scene to bend around a weakly related word.

## Standard German checks

- Enforce verb-second order in ordinary main clauses and correct word order in questions.
- Check articles, noun capitalisation, case, gender, verb agreement, separable verbs, negation,
  and polite forms.
- Prefer nominative and accusative constructions at this level. Use dative only in a common fixed
  phrase or when the everyday situation genuinely requires it.
- Replace uncertain or overly advanced language with a simpler, idiomatic alternative.
- Simplified pronunciation is a silent visual aid. It must be useful to an English speaker, but it
  is never part of the tutor narration.

## Retrieval and continuity

- The three Short items are the new focus items for the linked lesson and should not duplicate
  the most recently taught focus vocabulary.
- Recycle one or two previously taught words inside each long conversation when they fit naturally.
  Recycled words support spaced retrieval; they must not displace or obscure the three new items.
- Reuse prior material in a new context rather than repeating a previous scenario or copying its
  dialogue.
- Give the learner low-pressure repetition and useful success, not a test or a claim of mastery.

## Quality-review decision

The reviewer must correct the complete pack, not merely describe defects. Record a concise problem
for every material correction. A long lesson is not approved until its German-only sequence is a
complete, realistic A1 exchange and its English lines, focus vocabulary, and pronunciation guides
all agree with that corrected exchange.
